Reference cards

01 / Classification

Classify on different axes

MeaningExamples
Class: individual member of a classastronaut, sparrow
Collective: groupjury, swarm
Material: substanceclay, oxygen
Abstract: quality, process, ideacuriosity, recovery

Five senses: clay is perceptible but still a material noun. Separately check countability, number, structure and case. A collective noun is not automatically pluralia tantum.

03 / Greek & Latin plurals

Recognise the plural patterns

SingularPlural
stimulusstimuli
nucleusnuclei
bacteriumbacteria
analysisanalyses
diagnosisdiagnoses
formulaformulas / formulae

Patterns: -us → -i, -um → -a, -is → -es. Some classical and regular plurals coexist.

04 / Compound plurals

Find the head noun

PatternExample
Main noun inside compoundrunner-up → runners-up
Noun + prepositionfather-to-be → fathers-to-be
Final noun is the headraincoat → raincoats
No internal noun headgrown-up → grown-ups

Then form the genitive of the whole compound: runners-up’s medals; grown-ups’ tickets. Some lexicalised compounds must be checked in a dictionary.

05 / Genitive · Form

Choose the ending

Singular → ’sthe chef’s recipe
Plural ending in -s → ’the chefs’ recipes
Irregular plural → ’sthe women’s coats
Sibilant singular → usually ’sthe fox’s tail · the judge’s decision
Name ending in -s → style variesLucas’s bag (also Lucas’)
Compound → mark the whole compoundthe runners-up’s medals

First form the plural; then add the genitive ending. Sound matters: singular nouns ending /s, z, ʃ, ʒ, tʃ, dʒ/ usually take ’s.

06 / Genitive · Meaning

One relationship, several patterns

DependentOlivia’s notebook
Independent / absoluteThis notebook is Olivia’s.
EllipticalMine is newer than Ben’s [notebook].
Localat the florist’s [shop]
Double genitivea colleague of hers
Reciprocaleach other’s notes
Pronoun + elsesomeone else’s jacket
Inanimate nounthe tablet’s battery
Time / durationtwo weeks’ notice

Shared or separate possession?

SHARED · one thing togetherAva and Theo’s studioOnly the last name takes ’s. They share a studio.
SEPARATE · individual thingsAva’s and Theo’s studiosBoth names take ’s. Each has a studio.

The apostrophe pattern shows how the possessors are grouped; context determines whether the things are shared or separate.

’s or of? Which noun is already the topic? Which expression sounds less heavy? Both the tablet’s battery and the battery of the tablet are possible.

07 / Quantity & frequency expressions

Small patterns worth remembering

Frequency: a = pertwice a day · three times a week
Half + a/an + singular unithalf a litre · half an hour
A dozen = twelvea dozen postcards · half a dozen candles
Number + plural + ofdozens of postcards · hundreds of visitors
Duration genitivea day’s work · three days’ work
Measurement with a/an£4 a kilo · 60 miles an hour

Compare: twice a day (frequency) ≠ two days’ work (genitive of duration). Half a dozen means six; dozens of means many groups of twelve, not an exact number.
