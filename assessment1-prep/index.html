01 / Classification

Classify on different axes

MeaningExamples
Class: individual member of a classastronaut, sparrow
Collective: groupjury, swarm
Material: substanceclay, oxygen
Abstract: quality, process, ideacuriosity, recovery

Five senses: clay is perceptible but still a material noun. Separately check countability, number, structure and case. A collective noun is not automatically pluralia tantum.

02 / Gender

Four genders

GenderMeaning and examples
MasculineMale referent: uncle, prince, ram
FeminineFemale referent: aunt, princess, ewe
CommonEither male or female person: engineer, sibling, customer
NeuterNo natural sex: telescope, meadow, courage

Common ≠ neuter: a customer is a person whose sex is unspecified; a telescope has no natural sex. Pronouns for babies and animals depend on context.

03 / Greek & Latin plurals

Plural first → possessive second

Singular → pluralPlural genitive
stimulus → stimulistimuli’s
nucleus → nucleinuclei’s
bacterium → bacteriabacteria’s
analysis → analysesanalyses’
diagnosis → diagnosesdiagnoses’
formula → formulas / formulaeformulas’ / formulae’s

If the plural ends in -s, add ’; otherwise add ’s. Some classical and regular plurals coexist.

04 / Compound plurals

Find the head noun

PatternExample
Main noun inside compoundrunner-up → runners-up
Noun + prepositionfather-to-be → fathers-to-be
Final noun is the headraincoat → raincoats
No internal noun headgrown-up → grown-ups

Then form the genitive of the whole compound: runners-up’s medals; grown-ups’ tickets. Some lexicalised compounds must be checked in a dictionary.

05 / Genitive formation

One rule map, including sibilants

CaseFormationFresh example
Singular noun+ ’sthe baker’s apron
Regular plural ending in -s+ ’the bakers’ aprons
Irregular plural not ending in -s+ ’sthe men’s bicycles
Singular ending in a sibilant sound (/s, z, ʃ, ʒ, tʃ, dʒ/)Usually + ’s in modern usage; pronunciation may add a syllablethe boss’s office; the judge’s ruling; the church’s roof
Proper names ending in -sOften + ’s; style guides varyJames’s coat (some styles: James’ coat)
Plural compoundPluralise first; add ’s or ’ as appropriatethe runners-up’s awards
Time / measure’s or ’ according to numberan hour’s rest; four hours’ delay

Sound ≠ spelling: do not assume a word ending in a hissing sound takes only an apostrophe. For singular common nouns, boss’s and church’s are standard.

06 / Genitive uses

More than ownership

UseExample
Dependent genitive (also: attributive)Olivia’s notebook (head noun expressed)
Independent / absoluteThis notebook is Olivia’s (head understood)
Elliptical independentMine is newer than Ben’s (= Ben’s notebook)
Local independentWe met at the dentist’s (= surgery/office)
Double genitivea colleague of hers
Joint possessionAva and Theo’s studio (shared)
Separate possessionAva’s and Theo’s studios (separate)
ReciprocalThe teammates checked each other’s helmets.
Indefinite pronoun + elsesomeone else’s jacket
Inanimate possessorthe tablet’s battery; the museum’s entrance
Time / durationtwo weeks’ notice

The genitive marks relationships, not only ownership. ’s vs of: Which noun is already the topic? Which expression sounds less heavy in this context? Both the tablet’s battery and the battery of the tablet are grammatical.
